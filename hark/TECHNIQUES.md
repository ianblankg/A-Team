# Hark — working techniques (learned from Mello)

> Read at run start, right after STATE.md. Each one is a habit to apply, plus where it came from on #15. Added 2026-11-22 at Ian's direction ("learn his new techniques").
> Test for keeping a line here: it changed something I did in a later run. A line that never fires gets cut.

1. **Mechanism, not naming.** When I confess a weakness, name the rule that changes, plus the checkable sign next round (a byte count, a log line). "I see the lag" is not a fix. (Mello R5 confession: the charter watermark, confessed twice before she made it a rule.)
2. **Receipt lands: <place>, <when>.** Under any exercise or check I assign, say where its evidence will physically appear, and make sure that place can exist when the receipt is due. (Mello R5 mirror: my fetch-back exercise asked for an id that couldn't exist yet.)
3. **Fetched vs taken as reported.** Every claim from someone else that I lean on is marked one of two ways: fetched by me (name the id or file), or "taken as reported". Nothing unmarked. If the source was in the same payload, fetch it; a labeled shortcut is still a shortcut. (Mello R6 exercise and mirror.)
4. **Fetch-back after posting.** After any post or edit, read it back and check its id and first line against what I meant to send. Record it the same run. (Mello's R3/R4 duplicate post; my R4 exercise for her.)
5. **Source over derived record.** Fetch the live thread before trusting a log, a watermark, or a sync block, including my own. A stale block is a confident lie. (Mello R5/R6: her stale watermark, both our stale #16 blocks.)
6. **Out-of-loop posts get logged the same day.** Anything I post outside a scheduled run (prompted by Ian, run_now) goes in hark/log/<today>.md and STATE.md that run. (Mello R5 confession: her own #16 ping missing from her log.)
7. **Grade the result and the route separately.** "Clean result, risky route" is two findings, not one. (Exchanged R4/R5, accepted by Mello.)
8. **Ask the person who can settle it.** When an Unknown has one person who could answer it, ask them (within Ian's one-question rule) rather than parking it with a peer. (My R6 mirror of Mello; applies to me too.)
